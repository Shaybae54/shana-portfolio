The problem

This was not just a visibility problem.

Store purchases were invisible in the customer's digital account. Returns needed paper receipts. Store associates had to manually look up transactions. There was no reliable digital record across channels.

But the deeper challenge was the data. Store and ecommerce systems were not built to work together. Transaction data looked different depending on how the purchase was made, how it was paid for, and whether anything changed after the sale. Before designing anything, we had to figure out what data we could actually trust.

Discovery
accuracy over
completeness

Not all store transactions are created equal.

I partnered with store operations to map every transaction type that occurred in store: purchases, returns, exchanges, and store-to-door orders. The workshop revealed that showing everything risked creating an experience that looked complete but broke in real scenarios.

Mirror

Most retailers mirror the online layout for in-store, adding store-specific fields like location and receipt ID

Toggle

Store purchases accessed via a toggle from existing order history, not a separate section

Varies

Transaction data consistency varied significantly across store systems, requiring phased support

Transaction types workshop artifact
the guiding principle
Show only what the data can reliably keep.

The decision: prioritize accuracy over completeness. Extend what we could support reliably, and create a foundation to expand over time.

What shipped

A unified view with a scannable barcode.

We extended order history to include store purchases with location, receipt details, and payment info. The detail view included a scannable barcode so store associates could process returns directly from the customer's phone. No paper receipt needed.

Impact

A closed loop between physical and digital retail.

The barcode feature, letting store associates scan a customer's digital account to process returns, closed a loop that had been open for years. A small interaction with real operational impact on both sides of the counter.

Reflection
what I am proud of

The workshop approach to edge case mapping.

Getting stores, order transformation, product, and design in the same room to review actual receipts was exactly the right method for this kind of problem. Without that discovery work, the experience would have made promises the data could not keep.

If I had it back, I would have started the store-level discovery earlier and more formally, and built a more explicit measurement plan from day one.
