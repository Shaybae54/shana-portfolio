The problem

More reviews without making them meaningless.

Product pages had low review coverage, which limited customer confidence and merchandising insight. Marketing and UX partnered to explore whether loyalty points could increase participation without turning the reviews section into a points farm.

The challenge was not technically complex. It was strategically complex. How do you incentivize behavior without distorting it? That question shaped every decision from scope to launch.

Research
appreciation,
not payment

Customers were not disengaged. They just needed a reason.

I analyzed how other retailers incentivize reviews, focusing on whether incentives were transactional or loyalty-based, how they were communicated, and what guardrails protected quality.

Help

Customers leave reviews to help others or express strong feelings, not primarily for rewards

Framing

Incentives framed as appreciation drove better participation than those framed as payment

Lean

Competitors launched with minimal controls, learned from real behavior, then tightened guardrails

MoSCoW workshop artifact + competitor analysis
the core tension
Strong enough to change behavior, not so transactional it undermines trust.

The strategy: launch with enough structure to be credible, accept known risks intentionally, and use real behavior to inform what to tighten in future phases.

What shipped

50 loyalty points per approved review, layered into existing flows.

No new platform. Loyalty accounts matched via submitted email, no forced sign-in. Points awarded after moderation approval, independent of sentiment or rating. A "Reward Points Awarded" tag visible to all readers, transparent by design.

The anticipated quality tradeoff materialized exactly as expected. Both high-quality and low-quality reviews increased. The team had a real foundation for Phase 2 tightening.

Reflection
longer legs than the feature

The workshop process became a reference for the organization.

The MoSCoW model, bringing marketing, engineering, legal, and design into the same room before anyone wrote a spec, became a reference for how to run cross-functional alignment on ambiguous initiatives. That process had longer legs than the feature itself.

If I had it back, I would have introduced a soft character minimum earlier and tracked the quality ratio as a health metric from day one, not just total volume.

For the curious
Why we used BazaarVoice instead of building +
The existing review platform already had incentivized review capability. Using it meant updating content on the site rather than going through lengthy redesign and engineering. Work with what you have.
How legal shaped the transparency requirement +
Legal required clear disclosure when a reviewer received an incentive. Rather than treating this as a constraint, we made it a feature: the "Reward Points Awarded" tag became a trust signal.
What Phase 2 would tighten +
A soft character minimum to filter lowest-effort submissions. Quality ratio tracking as a health metric. And potentially tiered points for reviews with photos or detailed fit information.
