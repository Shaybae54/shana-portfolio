The problem

Customers had the credit. The experience made them forget it.

After a return, selecting instant store credit sent a gift card number and PIN by email. To use it later, customers had to remember it existed, find the email, copy both values, and type them in at checkout. Redemption was low, and the reason was not the offer.

The credit was not lost. The moment was.

Research across customer interviews, surveys, and data showed that value slipped through the cracks not because the credit was bad, but because it lived outside the shopping journey entirely.

The decision

What we chose and what we gave up

We could not change how the returns platform issued credit, add a new checkout step, or rely on customers remembering a PIN. The design had to work within those walls.

What we considered

  • Storing credit on account
  • Surfacing it in the shopping bag
  • Building a new wallet experience

What we rejected

  • Rebuilding gift card infrastructure
  • Creating a new account feature
  • A complex, multi-step flow

The tradeoff

  • Gift cards could not be stored on account or combined
  • So we separated awareness from action to work within the system

Reflection
what moved the number

The best fix was not a prettier screen. It was better timing.

It would have been easy to redesign the checkout surface. The leverage was in where and when the credit appeared. Placing value at the point of intent did more than any visual change could have.

If I had it back, I would involve support and CX teams earlier to quantify the cost of manual credit retrieval. That data would have earned the roadmap slot faster.

For the curious
Why reframing beat redesigning +
Treating it as a discoverability problem instead of a redemption problem changed the whole solution space. The fix lived in placement and timing, not in the offer itself.
Working within a platform I could not change +
The returns system issued credit a fixed way. The design met customers downstream in the bag and at checkout rather than fighting the source.
How the metrics were framed for the business +
Recovered value and incremental revenue mattered more to stakeholders than raw usage. Tying the outcome to dollars earned the roadmap slot.
What I would test next +
Whether surfacing credit even earlier, at product and category level, lifts usage further without feeling pushy.
